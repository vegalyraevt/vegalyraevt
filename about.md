<section class="section-shell section-block about-background" id="about-background" aria-labelledby="about-background-title">
  <div><p class="eyebrow">A practical starting point</p><h2 id="about-background-title">From Keeping Systems Running to Building New Ones.</h2></div>
  <div class="about-copy"><p>My background began in IT infrastructure and systems administration, learning how complicated technology behaves outside the lab: under load, across networks, and in the hands of real people. That foundation taught me to think in connected systems rather than isolated components.</p><p>It also taught me that software and hardware rarely behave as neatly as the diagrams suggest. Troubleshooting those problems turned into a broader interest in building systems instead of only maintaining them.</p><p>Now I'm studying mechatronics engineering and computer science, with mathematics supporting both. I'm moving from primarily digital infrastructure toward electronics, sensing, control, and physical systems, while continuing independent software and AI development.</p></div>
</section>

<section class="section-shell section-block about-principles" id="about-principles" aria-labelledby="about-principles-title">
  <div class="about-copy"><p class="eyebrow">Why I build</p><h2 id="about-principles-title">Because I Want to Know If It Can Be Done.</h2><p>A project often starts with an interesting question. I learn best by following it far enough to discover what I don't yet understand. Sometimes that becomes a working tool. Sometimes it stays an experiment. Both can teach me something useful.</p><p>The connecting thread is helping people express themselves, collaborate, and explore. Consent, provenance, and practical engineering constraints aren't obstacles to that goal; they make the work better.</p><blockquote>Technology should expand human creativity, not erase the people who make it possible.</blockquote></div>
  <dl class="about-principle-list">
    <div><dt>Build with, not from.</dt><dd>Consent and provenance are design requirements, not post-launch disclaimers.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Show the process.</dt><dd>Public development makes uncertainty visible and invites better questions.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Keep the system legible.</dt><dd>Complexity is sometimes necessary; obscurity is not. I want AI systems to be understandable and controllable.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Stay ambitious and specific.</dt><dd>Big visions become credible through concrete prototypes and honest status updates.</dd></div>
  </dl>
</section>
