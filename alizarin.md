<section class="section-shell section-block alizarin-split" id="alizarin-about" aria-labelledby="alizarin-about-title"><div class="alizarin-copy"><p class="eyebrow">What is ALIZARIN?</p><h2 id="alizarin-about-title">A Different Way to Give Characters a Voice.</h2><p>Many modern voice synthesis systems start with recordings of a human performer. ALIZARIN explores another path: constructing an entirely synthetic vocal source using computational methods, then developing that sound into a useful character voice.</p><p>Rather than copying an existing singer or voice actor, the goal is to design a new vocal identity with its own unusual characteristics. That creates opportunities for virtual singers, synthetic characters, speech systems, and experiments in expressive audio.</p></div>
  <dl class="alizarin-summary"><div><dt>Creator</dt><dd>Vega Lyrae</dd></div><div><dt>Umbrella project</dt><dd>Constellation Virtual Media</dd></div><div><dt>Primary focus</dt><dd>Synthetic voice technology</dd></div><div><dt>Development model</dt><dd>Public, open-source framework development</dd></div><div><dt>Voicebank status</dt><dd>Experimental beta access by inquiry</dd></div></dl>
</section>

<section class="section-shell alizarin-motivation" aria-labelledby="alizarin-motivation-title"><p class="eyebrow">A different starting point</p><h2 id="alizarin-motivation-title">No Human Voice Hidden Inside.</h2><div class="alizarin-copy"><p>I like virtual voices that sound like they belong to their own characters. That led me to explore making the source sound itself, instead of recording and imitating a human performer.</p><p>ALIZARIN explores how older synthesis techniques and modern machine learning might work together. The goal isn't to replace voice actors. It's to make another creative option available.</p><p class="alizarin-note">The <a href="https://github.com/ALIZARINENGINE/AlizarinEngine/blob/main/README.md">public README</a> acknowledges missile_39's Adachi Rei as an inspiration. Synthetic origins do not remove every licensing or misuse concern; responsible use and clear terms still matter.</p></div></section>

<section class="section-shell section-block alizarin-divider" id="alizarin-technology" aria-labelledby="alizarin-technology-title"><div class="section-heading"><p class="eyebrow">High-level technology overview</p><h2 id="alizarin-technology-title">From Synthetic Sound to a Virtual Voice.</h2><p>These are the project's publicly documented research areas, not a claim that every application has shipped.</p></div>
  <ol class="alizarin-technology" aria-label="Conceptual development workflow">
    <li><span class="alizarin-label">01 / Source sound</span><h3>Algorithmic Sound Generation</h3><p>ALIZARIN explores creating its initial sound from computational processes, including formant-inspired synthesis and layered sonic textures. Formants are resonances that help shape the character of a vocal sound.</p></li>
    <li><span class="alizarin-label">02 / Voice experiments</span><h3>Machine Learning and Voice Modeling</h3><p>The project investigates how synthetic sound can become more expressive and practical voice models, combining conventional synthesis concepts with newer machine learning techniques.</p></li>
    <li><span class="alizarin-label">03 / Creative applications</span><h3>Speech and Singing Applications</h3><p>The long-term framework is being designed around multiple ways to use a synthetic voice, including speech generation and vocal music production. Compatibility and individual capabilities remain in development.</p></li>
  </ol><p class="alizarin-note">A conceptual development workflow, not a completed production pipeline. See the <a href="https://github.com/ALIZARINENGINE/AlizarinEngine">repository</a> for public development information.</p>
</section>
